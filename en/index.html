Cross-database coverage

Discovery scale is not
a unique-paper total.

Scopus, Baidu Scholar, and Google Scholar use different document units and coverage boundaries. Their discovery-scale signals are shown side by side, but they are not added to DBLP until author identities, document types, and work versions have been reconciled.

    Data provenance

    Long-term maintenance begins with verifiability.

    01 / Identity baseline

    DBLP profile 40/3714 and DOI identifiers anchor identity and reduce homonym and duplicate-version errors.

    02 / Version relations

    DOI, DBLP key, and arXiv ID help identify versions of the same work. Record count is not a unique-work count.

    03 / Abstract policy

    Only source-verified abstracts are shown. Missing abstracts link to the original publication rather than inferred text.

    04 / Data releases

    Public records pass a strict field allowlist and source checks; the release date remains visible on the home page.

    05 / Two-level taxonomy

    Broad domains and tasks use explainable title and venue keywords for navigation, not an author-supplied classification.

    06 / Citation layer

    Citations come from Crossref Cited-by and are normalized within publication-year cohorts; they are not Google Scholar totals.

    07 / Database boundary

    Scopus and Baidu Scholar values remain expansion signals. Cross-database merging requires identity, document-type, and version reconciliation.

    08 / Chinese-language sources

    The DBLP corpus contains 453 conference records but no native Chinese-language conference metadata, reflecting DBLP’s international, English-language emphasis rather than an absence of Chinese outputs.
